## The search that wears people down

Ask anyone who has looked for a therapist how it went, and you'll usually hear a version of the same story. They opened a directory, filtered by what they thought mattered, and got hundreds of profiles that all looked alike. They sent a few messages. Some never got a reply. One called back a week later with a waitlist. Somewhere in there, the energy they'd gathered to ask for help ran out.

None of that is because the help isn't there. There are good providers in every state with room for new clients. The trouble is the path between the two: it's confusing, it's cold, and on a hard week it can be discouraging enough to make people stop.

PsychMind started with two people who see that path from both ends. One of us is a mental health provider and small practice owner. The other works behind the scenes supporting patients every day. We kept watching the same thing happen, so we decided to build the directory we kept wishing existed.

## What got lost as platforms grew

The big platforms solved a real problem: getting lots of providers into one place. But as they grew, something quieter got lost.

Independent providers became hard to see. A solo practice with years of experience could sit on page nine, behind whoever paid to be promoted. And the directories didn't reflect how providers actually practice: across several states, under several licenses, sometimes in two offices and online on the days in between. When a profile can only say one thing, it ends up saying the wrong thing to somebody.

For the person searching, that showed up as mismatches. Someone who could only meet online, in Florida, in the evenings, would find a perfect-sounding provider who turned out to be licensed somewhere else.

## Built around how providers really practice

So we started from the real shape of a practice, not from a form. On PsychMind, a provider lists every state they're licensed in, every place they see people, and whether they work online, in person or both. Search reads all of it. If you're looking for someone online in Florida, you only see providers who can see you there. If you'd rather sit in a room, you see the ones with a practice near you.

On your side of the search bar, we tried to make it feel less like a database and more like a conversation: how you'd like to meet, what's on your mind, and who feels right. You don't need to know the difference between an LMHC and an LCSW to begin.

## A few rules we won't bend

Every company says it cares. We'd rather write down the rules we've already built into PsychMind, so you can hold us to them.

- **Every provider is checked by a person.** Identity, licenses and credentials are reviewed by our team before a profile ever appears in a search.
- **You're never the product.** Providers pay for their listing, so there are no ads, no ad trackers, and we never sell your data.
- **A request stays between two people.** When you ask a provider for a session, only you and that provider can open it.
- **Help is always close.** Wherever someone in a hard moment might land, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is one tap away.

> Finding a provider should feel as safe as the therapy itself.
>
> The PsychMind team

## Where we are now

We're early, and we'd rather say so. The directory grows one verified provider at a time, and we'll keep writing here about what we build, what we get wrong, and what we change because of it.

If you're a provider who has felt invisible on the big platforms, we'd love to have you. And if you're looking for someone to talk to, we hope this is the last search you have to do.
