## The question I hear most

Before a first session, almost everyone asks me some version of the same thing: is this serious enough? Am I taking a spot from someone who needs it more?

Many people wait until things feel unbearable, as if therapy were only for a breaking point. It isn't. You don't need a diagnosis or a crisis to deserve help. If something in your life feels heavy, stuck or confusing, that's reason enough to talk to someone.

## Signs it might be time

You don't need all of these. Even one can be enough.

- **You keep landing in the same patterns.** You react, think or behave in ways you don't like, even when you're trying to change.
- **Your feelings are harder to manage than usual.** Stress, worry, sadness or anger take up more room than they used to.
- **Daily life is starting to give.** Sleep, work, relationships or motivation are slipping.
- **You've tried handling it alone.** Talking to friends, journaling or reading helped a little, but not enough.
- **You'd simply like to understand yourself better.** You're not in crisis. You're curious, and that counts too.

## What happens in a first session

A first session is mostly a conversation about you. I'll ask what brought you in, a little about your life, and what you'd like to be different. You'll have time to ask me questions too: how I work, what sessions look like, what I'd suggest.

You don't have to tell your whole story at once, and "I'm not ready to talk about that yet" is a perfectly good answer. Think of it as two people getting to know each other, not a test you can fail.

By the end, most people have a rough sense of whether we're a good fit and what we might work on together.

## How therapy feels over time

Progress is rarely a straight line. Some sessions bring relief. Some feel uncomfortable, because you're looking at things you usually step around, and that's often where change happens. Early on, the shifts are small enough that you may only notice them looking back.

A few months in, people tell me things like: I caught myself before the spiral. I said no without apologizing. I slept through the night. Those are the moments that add up.

## Fit matters most

Research and experience agree on this: the relationship between you and your provider is one of the strongest predictors of whether therapy helps. Look for someone you feel comfortable with, whose way of talking suits you, and who has experience with what you're facing.

If it doesn't feel right after a few sessions, it's okay to say so, or to try someone else. A good provider won't take it personally. They want you to get the help you came for.

> Starting therapy isn't a sign that something is wrong with you. It's a sign that you're paying attention.
>
> Dr. Danny Schwelberg

## A final thought

You don't need permission to start therapy. If something feels off, that's enough.

And if you're in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, don't wait for an appointment: call or text 988, any time, day or night.
