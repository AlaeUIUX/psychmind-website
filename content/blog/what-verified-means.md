## A badge is a promise

Next to every provider's name on PsychMind there's a small blue seal. Making a badge is easy. Making one that means something is harder, and the only way we know to do it is to say exactly what it stands for.

So here it is, step by step: what we check, who checks it, and what happens after.

## A person, not a bot

When a provider joins, they build their profile and send it to us. Before anyone can find them, a person on our team reviews it. Not a script that looks for filled-in fields: a person who opens the documents and compares them with what the profile says.

That review covers three things.

**Identity.** Every provider confirms who they are with a government-issued ID.

**Licenses and credentials.** License numbers, degrees and qualification documents are reviewed before a profile goes live.

**Practice details.** Where they practice, how they see people and what they charge, so what you read on a profile is what you get.

If something doesn't match, we don't guess. The profile goes back to the provider with a note explaining what's missing, and they can fix it and send it again.

## Licenses, state by state

Mental health licenses are issued by states, and a provider can only see clients in the states where they're licensed. That's as true on a video call as it is in a room.

So we verify licenses one state at a time, and our search follows them. A practice location only shows on a profile in a state where the provider has a verified license. When a provider adds a new state, it waits until that license has been checked. Until then, nobody searching there sees them.

It's a small rule with a big effect: you'll never fall for a profile only to find out the provider can't see you where you live.

## When something changes

Verification isn't a single moment. People change their names, open new practices and add licenses. Most changes go straight to the profile. The ones that matter for trust come back to us first.

If a verified provider changes their name, professional title, business name, NPI or a license, their profile keeps showing what we've already verified until our team has reviewed the change. Everything else, like their bio, their photo or the specialties they list, updates the moment they save it.

## What verified doesn't mean

We want to be just as clear about the limits.

Verified means a provider is who they say they are and holds the licenses and credentials they list. It doesn't mean they'll be the right fit for you. Fit is personal: how someone listens, how they explain things, whether you leave a session feeling a little lighter. No review of documents can promise that.

That's why we'd always suggest treating a first session as a conversation, and why it's fine to try someone else if it doesn't feel right. The seal is where trust starts. The rest is between the two of you.
